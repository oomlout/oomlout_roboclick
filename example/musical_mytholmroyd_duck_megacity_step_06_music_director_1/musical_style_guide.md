# Musical Style Guide — Mytholmroyd Duck Megacity
Status: DRAFT · step_06

## Sound World
A compact electronic band makes the valley feel ready to move: clean plucked synths, rounded bass, dry programmable drums, a few broad bright pads reserved for the final reveal. Keep the vocal centre close and legible. The metro is in the regularity and momentum of the pulse, not in train sound effects through every song. Mabel’s plucked colour suggests folk practice without leaving the shared electronic idiom; Elsie’s gentleness still has a living pulse underneath.

## Rhythmic Identity
The duck begins with a small repeated figure that closes before it can get anywhere. Scene 3 runs speech subdivisions against a fixed beat until the line of thought loses its listeners. Scene 4 learns to leave space; thereafter each principal sets a different relation to the beat: Mabel measured, Nora syncopated, Asha spacious, Elsie unhurried. Scene 9 coordinates those separate identities through a passed work rhythm. Scene 10 can be broad because the listener has heard the small spaces that earned it.

## Harmonic Language
Use clear tonal centres, pentatonic directness, suspended seconds and warm sixths. Minor territory in scenes 3–4 marks discomfort, not a villain or disaster. The broad D-major home of the opener and finale changes its meaning through phrase length rather than a gratuitous final key change. Specific keys below are composition suggestions and may be transposed to actual singers without changing the dramatic function.

## Recurring Motifs
The circuit: four compact notes returning early to home, scene 1; in scene 10 the phrase continues through the bar it formerly used as a stop. The space: an uncovered beat first felt painfully in scene 3, chosen deliberately in scene 4 and made comfortable in scene 8. The gate: a clear accented gesture passed between voices in scene 9, answered by the opening pulse of scene 10. None requires copying a source lyric fragment.

## Character Musical Signatures
canal_duck: clipped light tenor phrases learn longer listening rests; the finale extends, rather than replaces, his contour. mabel_lockwood: dry exact mezzo questions articulate firm pulse points and earn a sustained assent. nora_greenwood: airy high phrases change direction before returning to a simple line. asha_rahman: low intimate phrases leave room around the voice, with one controlled lift. elsie_sutcliffe: grounded low phrases sit behind the beat without slowing the whole score. miriam_vale: full clear legato occupies a brief recognition passage, then yields space.

## Act Arc
Act 1 presses, breaks, listens and finally earns a firm voluntary cadence in Mabel’s number. Act 2 restarts brightly at Nora’s doorstep, then makes room for two quieter decisions before shared construction energy. The finale opens large, reduces scale for recognition and identity, and returns to breadth with a complete cadence. Do not keep raising volume from song to song: different kinds of attention are the score’s movement.
