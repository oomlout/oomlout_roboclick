# Mytholmroyd Duck Megacity — story guide
Status: DRAFT · step_01 · 2026-09-06

## The Show
A small duck gives a whole valley permission to want a bigger life. Make a warm, funny, sung-through pop/electronic musical in which housing, trains and a park become personal: visiting somebody without arranging a lift, leaving home without losing it, choosing one's own front door. The utopia is sincere. Its only failed pitch teaches Canal Duck to listen; it does not disprove his vision. Let the audience enjoy the enormous confidence of a very small campaigner, then discover how much better he gets when he stops talking.

## The Protagonist's Journey
1. **A life too small.** At the canal bank at dawn, Canal Duck tests the boundaries of a life assigned by his name. Set up the departed mayor by role only. His desire grows; his identity is not yet resolved.
2. **Room for another use.** On the narrow strip between canal and River Calder, he physically tries the river and discovers it supplies a duck's needs. The adjacent canal corridor could do a different job. Preserve the river; leave the engineering idea for scene 3.
3. **The pitch he cannot take back.** Outside the village hall after a failed presentation, he enters a sung flashback: watch him present the park, buried double-stacked express metro and affordable towers to a silent unnamed audience, then watch that audience reject his approach. Return to the same nervous aftermath. He does not merely sing that it happened. No named interaction and no rendered future city in the flashback.
4. **Make room for an answer.** Inside the hall, the four principals voice their different attachments in short alternating sections. He abandons presenting at them and commits to listening individually. This is the same setback's resolution, not a second rejection.
5. **Mabel's exact question.** At a lock gate, Mabel examines what happens to the waterways she values. The existing proposal's access and protection funding persuade her; respect is concrete, not a slogan. Her assent belongs to her.
6. **Nora counts the visits.** On her lane, the friendly, flighty resident discovers how much of her day disappears into arranging car journeys. The connected flat and linear park let her keep people close. Give this vignette a different form from Mabel's.
7. **Asha keeps a return route.** On the existing station platform, Asha learns that leaving to study need not mean surrendering home. Affordable space and work make a return possible. She chooses opportunity without promising to hurry back; her actual departure comes after the communal start of work.
8. **Elsie chooses for herself.** In her front room, the elder sees that warmth, a lift and accessible transit can preserve her freedom. Understate the duck. She makes the decisive statement; nobody infantilizes her or tells her independence is over.
9. **The first gate gives.** The duck and four principals begin the work together. Mabel leads respectful sequential draining; wildlife is welcomed into the untouched river. Show construction only, never a train or finished tower. Every conversion has already happened. Close with intentions to build and to leave, carrying us over the coming years.
10. **The place can hold them.** A new dawn after a few years. The first double-stacked train arrives at the opening of ONE continuous finale; Asha steps off. Reveal massive modern public-housing towers against the Calder Valley landform. Miriam Vale then enters and is named for the first time, recognizing the community's work. Canal Duck finally loosens the link between his name and his address. Finish triumphant and curious, with a life still to explore.

## The Characters
**Canal Duck** is the only named animal. His cartoon-style generic British voice contrasts with the humans. He must learn a listening technique in scene 4 and a freedom of identity in scene 10: those are different changes.

**Mabel Lockwood**, human, is a mid-50s local canal expert. Her precise questions prevent the show's affection for progress from becoming contempt for restoration work. Her hands opening a gate in scene 9 are the visible proof that her scene-5 assent meant something.

**Nora Greenwood**, human, is friendly and flighty. She values familiar people and discovers that the means of seeing them matter more than the old arrangement of buildings. Her comic changes of direction should sharpen, rather than blur, her eventual choice.

**Asha Rahman**, human, is serious and soft-spoken. She needs room to grow away from the valley and a viable way home. Her quiet scene-7 decision earns the spectacular homecoming at the opening of the finale.

**Elsie Sutcliffe**, human, has a deep, deliberately cliché Yorkshire voice. She is protecting authority over her own day. Accessibility offers her more authority; write her conversion as a decision she owns, not a kindly intervention.

**Miriam Vale**, human, is the mayor who left for a larger job. Her earlier belief is backstory, never a new staged encounter. She appears only in scene 10. Her recognition confirms what the others achieved; she does not deliver money, permission or a rescue.

## The World
Present-day Mytholmroyd shares its daily life with unnamed animal neighbours. For scenes 1–8 hold the same late-spring/summer season and recognizable existing places. Scene 9 alone contains construction. Scene 10 alone contains the completed metro and city. The River Calder remains wild and unharmed. The visual language is simple, bold chibi vector illustration; contemporary transport and towers use clean modern surfaces without heritage masonry. The enormous scale belongs to the finale, while the human conversations stay intimate.

## What Makes This Specific
The campaigner is named after the very thing he proposes to remove. Two waterways already occupy the same narrow valley; the river answers his personal needs while the former transport corridor gains a new public purpose. Mabel does not have to stop loving canals. Nora does not have to stop loving neighbours. Asha does not have to choose between learning and belonging. Elsie does not have to accept dependence. Let a halted pitch be awkward enough to feel, then let the scale of the finale feel earned by four very small acts of attention.
